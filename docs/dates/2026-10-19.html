Praxisforum Tiefengeothermie highlights achievements of the German geothermal sector

Germany's geothermal industry is expanding significantly, as demonstrated at a major conference where operators reported that geothermal plants produced 3.8 terawatt-hours (TWh) of heat nationwide in 2025, enough to supply a large city. The sector is increasingly focused on district heating networks (pipes that distribute hot water from geothermal sources to multiple buildings), with geothermal heating prices running about 16% cheaper than the national average for district heating. Several award-winning plants showed strong performance, and new drilling projects are underway across the country to meet growing demand.

COP31 a chance for Türkiye to put geothermal on the agenda

Türkiye is hosting the COP31 climate conference in 2026, which gives the country a chance to showcase its geothermal energy success to world leaders and investors. Türkiye is a global leader in geothermal power (a renewable energy source that taps heat from deep underground), ranking fourth worldwide and first in Europe, and uses it not just for electricity but also for heating buildings and agriculture. The country's experience could provide valuable lessons for other regions developing geothermal projects, though challenges like high upfront costs and long permitting processes need government support to overcome.

Temperature field and assessment methodology for a low-medium temperature geothermal field: 3D geological-geophysical modeling in the Xiong'an Area, Northern China

Researchers in northern China created a three-dimensional computer model to understand heat distribution in a geothermal field (an underground area with naturally hot rock). They combined geological data (information about rock layers) and geophysical data (measurements of physical properties underground) to map temperatures across the region. This modeling approach helps assess whether a geothermal area is suitable for energy development.

▲ Drilling contract signed for IDDP-3 superhot geothermal well in Iceland

Iceland has signed a contract to drill an exploratory well as part of the IDDP-3 project, which focuses on superhot rock geothermal technology—extracting energy from extremely hot rock deep underground. This is the third major phase of a research project that has been developing deep geothermal drilling techniques for over 25 years, building on knowledge from two previous wells. The project aims to test and develop ways to access geothermal energy at temperatures above 400°C (around 750°F), which could revolutionize energy production worldwide.

▲ CeraPhi Energy to evaluate geothermal repurposing of Star Energy oil and gas wells in the UK

CeraPhi Energy and Star Energy have partnered to convert old oil and gas wells in the UK into geothermal energy systems that produce renewable heat. CeraPhi will use its proprietary CeraPhiWell technology, which circulates special fluid down existing wells to extract heat from hot rock below ground, creating a closed system that can operate for at least 50 years. The deal targets up to 25 wells and represents a way to reuse existing infrastructure rather than drilling new wells, reducing costs and environmental impact of geothermal development.

▲ Quaise Energy closes $134m Series B funding round to support superhot geothermal project

Quaise Energy raised $134 million in funding to develop Project Obsidian, a superhot geothermal power plant in Oregon that taps into extremely hot rock deep underground. The first phase aims to generate 50 megawatts of electricity by 2030, with plans to expand to 250 megawatts.

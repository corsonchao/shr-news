▲ Quaise raises $180 million in final close of Series B to fund superhot geothermal project

▲ Geothermal drilling by Aardwarmte Wippolderlaan in the Netherlands completed

A geothermal project in the Netherlands has successfully completed drilling two wells nearly 2.4 kilometers deep into rock layers called Delft Sandstone, which will be used to extract heat from the earth for heating homes and businesses. The wells reached a temperature of 85°C and are positioned about one kilometer apart—one will pull hot water up (production well) and the other will return cooled water back down (reinjection well). The team now plans to install surface equipment like pipes and heat exchangers to transfer this geothermal heat to the local heating network, with heat delivery expected to begin in mid-2028.

▲ Study finds geothermal learning remains highly local

A new study analyzing 514 geothermal power plants over 70 years found that costs have actually increased rather than decreased as more geothermal capacity was built globally, unlike solar and wind which get cheaper with scale. The research shows that geothermal knowledge (understanding of underground water systems, drilling techniques, and local regulations) stays within specific regions and doesn't transfer easily between countries, because each location has unique geology and regulatory requirements. The study suggests that successful geothermal markets need continuous project pipelines and stable policies to retain specialized expertise, rather than relying on occasional large projects.

▲ Start of drilling imminent for geothermal heating project in Gräfelfing, Germany

A German town called Gräfelfing is preparing to drill two deep wells into underground rock layers about 2,900 meters below the surface to access hot water for heating. The hot water, which will be around 100°C (212°F), will be used to heat homes and buildings through a district heating network (a system that distributes heat from a central source to multiple buildings), and the cooled water will be pumped back underground. If successful, the project could provide up to 25 megawatts of thermal energy (a measure of heat power) and begin supplying heat by 2029.

▲ Endurance Energy demonstrates subsea geothermal power in Pacific Ocean

A company called Endurance Energy successfully tested an underwater geothermal power system at a volcanic seamount 300 miles off Oregon's coast. The system drilled into the ocean floor and used hot water from deep-sea hydrothermal vents (natural hot springs) to generate electricity, producing 16.5 kilowatts of power, though only briefly. While the test had technical issues, it proves that underwater geothermal power generation is possible, and the company plans to develop commercial systems and deploy them near Tonga by 2027.

Seismic surveys ongoing for geothermal exploration in Baja, Hungary

Hungary's government is using seismic surveys (sound waves sent into the earth to map underground structures) to explore whether the Baja region has hot water and rock suitable for geothermal energy production. If successful, the hot water could heat homes and buildings, replace some natural gas use, and potentially generate electricity. This exploration is part of a national program to expand geothermal energy use across Hungary.

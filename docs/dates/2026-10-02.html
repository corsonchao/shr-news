▲ Drilling and testing of geothermal wells in Geiselbullach, Germany completed

A geothermal heating project in Germany has successfully completed drilling two wells that will supply hot water to a district heating network (a system that distributes heat from a central source to multiple buildings). The wells produced water at 79-80°C with a flow rate of 90 liters per second, meeting the project's target of generating 10 megawatts of heat. The system should begin supplying heat to customers by the 2027 heating season and will provide an environmentally friendlier alternative to the region's previous reliance on waste incineration for heating.

▲ Fervo Energy announces commercial operation of first geothermal power plant in Cape Station, Utah

Fervo Energy has successfully started generating electricity at its first geothermal power plant in Utah, marking a major milestone as the world's first commercial enhanced geothermal system (a technique that creates artificial hot rock reservoirs deep underground). The plant produced power just 23 months after construction began, much faster than typical large energy projects. Fervo plans to expand this site significantly with additional power blocks coming online over the next few years.

  • 33 megawatt initial unit capacity

▲ From Underground Heat to Reliable Power: Engineering Geothermal for the Field

Next-generation geothermal power plants are moving from laboratory concepts to real-world operation, and success depends on much more than just drilling deep wells. The critical challenge is engineering the surface equipment—pumps, turbines, and seals—that converts underground heat into reliable electricity over many years. This requires careful selection of components that work together as a system, thorough testing before installation, and ongoing monitoring to catch problems early rather than during a costly shutdown.

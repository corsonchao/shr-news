▲ Ennatuurlijk Aardwarmte to decommission geothermal wells for the first time in the Netherlands

A Dutch geothermal company is permanently closing three heat-extraction wells in Middenmeer after 15 years of operation—a first for the Netherlands. The wells failed sooner than the expected 30-year lifespan, but the company learned valuable lessons about well design, materials, and safety monitoring that are now being applied to new projects. As the company goes through the full lifecycle of a geothermal installation for the first time, they are carefully developing best practices for safely decommissioning wells, which helps ensure the geothermal industry matures into a responsible sector.

▲ Measurement phase of large-scale 3D seismic survey in Munich, Germany completed

A major survey in the Munich area of Germany has finished collecting underground rock data using seismic imaging (a technique that uses vibrations to map what's beneath the surface). Over three months, researchers deployed sensitive listening devices called geophones across 1,100 square kilometers to understand the rock layers and hot water reservoirs that could be used for geothermal energy projects. The collected data will now be combined with existing information to create a detailed 3D map of the underground, which will help plan future geothermal drilling sites in the region.

▲ Gravity-driven flow dispersion and stiffness-constrained fracture deformation mitigate flow channeling in vertical fracture enhanced geothermal systems

Researchers studied how water flows through cracks in hot rock layers used for geothermal energy. They found that gravity (the natural downward pull on fluids) and the stiffness (resistance to bending) of the surrounding rock help prevent water from taking shortcuts through certain cracks, which would waste energy. This discovery could improve how geothermal systems work by ensuring heat is captured more efficiently.

Xiong’an New Area, China facility identified as national-level shallow geothermal pilot

China has designated a large heating and cooling system in Xiong'an New Area as a national pilot project for shallow geothermal energy, which uses heat from underground rock at moderate depths. The facility uses 2,090 vertical wells drilled about 600 meters deep, combined with ground source heat pumps (devices that move heat from the ground to buildings), to provide heating, cooling, and hot water to over 350,000 square meters of buildings. The project demonstrates how geothermal systems can be built in a repeatable, modular way and monitored with intelligent software to efficiently manage energy supply.

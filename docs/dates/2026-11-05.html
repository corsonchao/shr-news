▲ France suspends Heat Fund applications amidst gap between budget and aid requests

▲ Induced seismicity at Rittershoffen geothermal plant, France studied as operators aim to resume

A geothermal heating plant in France that has been operating since 2016 was shut down in December 2025 after experiencing over 400 small earthquakes (induced seismicity—earthquakes triggered by human activity) in two weeks, with the largest measuring 2.5 magnitude. Scientists determined that the earthquakes were caused by thermal contraction—the rock shrinking as it cooled from the plant's heat extraction process—rather than by pressure from fluid injection alone. Computer models predict that if the plant operates for another 20 years, the cooling effect could release 10 times more seismic energy, potentially causing a magnitude 3.1 earthquake, though operators are exploring technical modifications to restart the plant safely by year-end.

Drilling commences for cold district heating network in Münster, Germany

A city utility company in Germany is drilling shallow wells about 300 meters deep to gather information about underground temperatures and soil types for a heating and cooling system called a cold district heating network. This system uses heat pumps (machines that move heat from one place to another) to extract warmth from the ground and provide heating in winter or cooling in summer to buildings. The city has already successfully built similar systems in two other neighborhoods and is also exploring deeper drilling down to 1,500 meters to reach hotter rock layers by 2028.

▲ Green Therma signs drilling contract for closed-loop geothermal demonstration in Denmark

Green Therma has hired a drilling company to build a deep geothermal well in Denmark as part of a demonstration project for their Heat4Ever technology, which is a closed-loop system (meaning water circulates in a sealed loop underground and transfers heat without escaping) that extracts heat from hot rock to supply district heating. The well will reach about 4 kilometers straight down and then 3 kilometers horizontally, making it Denmark's deepest geothermal well, with drilling expected to start in January 2027 and funded by approximately $11 million from Denmark's government.

Researchers in Germany are studying whether limestone rock formations in the Rhine-Ruhr region are suitable for extracting geothermal heat (thermal energy from deep underground). The three-year research project will map suitable locations, test how rock and fluids interact, and create computer tools to help energy companies plan and evaluate geothermal projects in the region.

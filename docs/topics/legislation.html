▲ Geothermal exploration permit in Guadeloupe granted to Albioma

A French energy company called Albioma has received permission to explore for geothermal resources (heat from deep underground rock) at a site in Guadeloupe. Over the next five years, they will study existing geological data, conduct surface surveys, and create models to determine if there is a high-temperature geothermal reservoir (an underground area containing hot rock suitable for energy production) in the region. The company will need to apply separately for drilling permission if their exploration is successful.

Philippine economy council approves $170m geothermal de-risking facility

The Philippines has approved a $170 million government fund to help reduce financial risk for companies exploring geothermal energy (heat from within the Earth used to generate electricity). The fund will cover at least half the costs of geothermal drilling and exploration, with companies only repaying the government if their exploration finds economically viable resources. This initiative aims to boost geothermal development in the Philippines, which currently ranks third globally in geothermal power capacity but has seen slower growth compared to neighboring countries.

Exploration permit granted for Barranquero geothermal site in Colombia

Colombia's government has approved a five-year exploration permit for the El Barranquero geothermal site, which will allow companies to drill deep wells, study underground rock formations, and assess the area's potential for generating heat energy from the earth. The project is expected to cost approximately $11.8 million and is part of the San Diego geothermal region, which experts estimate could produce 133.79 MW (a measure of electrical power output). This is the third major geothermal exploration project approved in Colombia recently, showing growing interest in this renewable energy source.

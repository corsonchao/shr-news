Next-generation geothermal power plants are moving from laboratory concepts to real-world operation, and success depends on much more than just drilling deep wells. ▲ Alberta’s deep drilling expertise targets the next frontier in geothermal energy

Alberta's oil and gas drilling expertise is being redirected toward superhot rock geothermal energy, which taps into heat deep underground to generate electricity or provide heating. Four companies received $20.8 million in funding to develop technologies that make geothermal drilling cheaper and easier, including an electric drilling robot and sensors that can withstand temperatures above 200°C. While Alberta's geology isn't ideal for geothermal itself, the province's deep drilling skills position it to export these technologies globally, similar to how Iceland developed geothermal but now needs expertise for deeper, harder-to-reach heat sources.

▲ Kyoto Fusioneering to supply gyrotron for Quaise’s superhot geothermal drilling technology

Kyoto Fusioneering has agreed to supply a gyrotron—a specialized microwave device—to Quaise for their superhot rock geothermal drilling technology. A gyrotron is a high-powered electronic tool that generates intense microwave energy to help drill into extremely hot rock layers deep underground, which is a key innovation for accessing geothermal heat at greater depths than traditional drilling methods.

▲ Hephae successfully deploys MWD system at 210 ºC circulation temperature in geothermal well

A company called Hephae has successfully tested a specialized tool called Pandora210 that measures and guides drilling while equipment is deep underground in a geothermal well, operating at temperatures of 210°C (about 410°F). This tool is designed to withstand the extreme heat and vibrations that occur when drilling into very hot rock formations deep below the surface, which is essential for superhot rock geothermal energy projects. The achievement is important because reliable tools at these temperatures help reduce expensive drilling delays and make geothermal energy more economical.

▲ Exergy launches US operations to support rapid geothermal growth across North America

Exergy, a company that makes turbine systems for converting heat into electricity, is opening a US headquarters in Houston to support the growing geothermal energy industry in North America. The company has won an award for its Gemini turbine, which is designed to work with Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS)—a method of creating geothermal resources by fracturing hot rock deep underground—and can generate up to 60 megawatts of power from a single unit, making geothermal plants simpler and cheaper to build.

Overall performance analysis of a stackable thermoelectric generator for medium-low temperature geothermal resources

Researchers have developed and analyzed a stackable thermoelectric generator (a device that converts heat directly into electricity) designed to work with medium-low temperature geothermal resources (heat from the Earth that is moderately warm rather than extremely hot). This study examines how well the generator performs when multiple units are stacked together, which could make geothermal energy more efficient for locations where the underground heat isn't extremely hot.

▲ Hephae successfully deploys MWD system at 210 ºC circulation temperature in geothermal well

A company called Hephae has successfully tested a specialized tool called Pandora210 that measures and guides drilling while equipment is deep underground in a geothermal well, operating at temperatures of 210°C (about 410°F). This tool is designed to withstand the extreme heat and vibrations that occur when drilling into very hot rock formations deep below the surface, which is essential for superhot rock geothermal energy projects. The achievement is important because reliable tools at these temperatures help reduce expensive drilling delays and make geothermal energy more economical.

Smart geothermal power plant management demonstrated in Taiwan

A geothermal power plant in Taiwan tested new smart management technologies using 5G networks (wireless communication) and artificial intelligence (computer systems that learn and make decisions). ▲ NLR highlights licensable technologies for deeper, more profitable geothermal drilling

The National Laboratory of the Rockies has identified three ready-to-use technologies that could make geothermal drilling deeper and more cost-effective. These include a high-temperature power generator that works in extreme heat, specialized sensors and electronics that collect drilling data in harsh conditions, and software that predicts costs for new geothermal projects. These innovations are already available for geothermal companies to license and use.

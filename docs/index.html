▲ Deep geothermal exploration campaign in Berlin, Germany officially launched

A German company called BEW has started exploring whether deep geothermal energy—heat extracted from hot rock layers far underground—can power Berlin's heating system. The project, called Geo4Berlin, will drill down nearly 2 kilometers into the earth to test rock formations that might hold hot water, measure temperatures, and understand the rock properties. If successful, they plan to deliver geothermal-heated water to Berlin homes and buildings starting in the early 2030s.

▲ A novel enhanced open-loop geothermal system with a single horizontal well: Heat extraction performance improved by liquid nitrogen stimulation

Researchers developed a new approach to extract heat from underground rock using a single horizontal well (a borehole drilled sideways through the earth rather than straight down). They enhanced the system's performance by using liquid nitrogen, an extremely cold substance, to create small fractures in the rock that allow heat to be extracted more effectively. This open-loop design means water or another fluid circulates through the rock to absorb heat without a closed recycling system.

Webinar – Construction of borehole heat exchanger using steel shot drilling, 6 November 2026

A company called Canopus Drilling Solutions has developed a new drilling method using steel shot (small metal pellets) that can create deep boreholes (holes drilled into the ground) for heat exchangers—systems that extract heat from underground rocks. These boreholes are much more efficient than traditional shallow ones, producing ten times more heating and cooling per year, and can be drilled quickly using a small truck-mounted machine that fits in urban spaces like parking lots. The technology works through both soft soil and very hard rock, making it potentially useful for both regular heating/cooling systems and deeper geothermal energy projects.
